What you'll learn

Six things you will be able to do afterwards

Not "understand" or "appreciate" — do, on Monday, in the file you already have open.

01

Pick a typeface you can defend

Work from the brief and the content rather than the mood board, and say out loud why this one and not that one.

02

Build a scale that holds up

Set a modular scale in code, then know exactly where to break it when a real screen demands it.

03

Set long text properly

Measure, leading, hyphenation and the punctuation you have been getting wrong since school.

04

Make dense screens readable

Tables, labels, numerals and truncation — where typography stops being decoration and starts being load-bearing.

05

Meet accessibility properly

Contrast against strokes not boxes, spacing requirements, and reflow at 400% without a horizontal scrollbar.

06

Ship it and keep it

Subsetting, fallback stacks that do not shift the layout, and writing the type section of a design system.

Do I need design training to take this?

No. It assumes you can already build or design an interface and that nobody ever taught you type. Engineers take it as often as designers do, and the code sections use plain CSS.

How long does it actually take?

Around eleven and a half hours of material, and most people finish in six to ten weeks. The planner above turns your available hours into a real date rather than making you guess.

Is there a deadline?

No. Access does not expire, the exercises have no due dates, and nothing is drip-fed. If you disappear for four months your ticks are still here.

Will I get feedback on the exercises?

On the final project, yes — a written critique within ten working days. The other exercises are self-directed with a worked example to compare against.

What if it isn't for me?
